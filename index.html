Accessibility & Dyslexia Reading Guide

Modern websites often pack bright white backgrounds, dense columns, and moving ads onto a single screen. For people with dyslexia, visual stress, ADHD, low vision, or age-related eye fatigue, a few practical adjustments make reading dramatically easier:

  • Lower Glare with Dark Slate or Tinted Backgrounds: Pure white screens can create harsh contrast ("glare") that causes words to shimmer. A calm Dark Slate background paired with Crisp White or Warm Cream text reduces eye strain during longer reading sessions.
  • Increase Letter & Line Spacing: Adding gentle space between letters and lines prevents characters from crowding together, helping your eyes track smoothly from the end of one line to the start of the next.
  • Use a Reading Spotlight: Dimming surrounding paragraphs or completed recipe steps to 35% opacity keeps your eyes anchored on one clear block at a time—especially helpful in the kitchen when glancing back and forth from a mixing bowl.
  • Combine Sight with Native Read Aloud: Listening while following the highlighted step reinforces comprehension and lets you cook hands-free at your own pace.
